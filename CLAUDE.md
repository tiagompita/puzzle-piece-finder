# Puzzle Piece Finder

A computer-vision tool for a physical jigsaw (Clementoni 3000 "The Alps", ref. 33545; grid 64 × 47 = 3008 pieces, 218 border pieces, nominal cell ≈ 18.5 × 17.9 mm). Canonical input is a **flatbed scan at 300 dpi** of loose pieces on saturated-colour paper, spaced ≥ 5 mm (absolute scale: 1 px = 0.0847 mm). CLI (`src/main.py`, naive only) and a Tkinter GUI (`src/gui.py`).

**Symmetry (verified 2026-10-30):** the grid is 64 × 47 (confirmed by counting) and the die is symmetric under a 180° rotation: piece (l, c) has the same cut as piece (48 − l, 65 − c), rotated 180°, giving 1504 twin pairs and no self-twin. Measured on 20 border pairs: twin RMS 0.29 mm (same pose) / 0.39 mm (opposite pose) against 0.27 mm for a re-scan of the same piece and ≥ 0.60 mm for the nearest non-twin (`docs/diagnostico/gemeos/RESULTADOS.md`). Twins are physically interchangeable (one fits where the other belongs), so **only the image tells them apart: the image tie-break is mandatory**. Assembled sections: the front scanned at 300 dpi is enough; the back is optional (only 65–70 % of the cut length shows as a dark line there, the rest is relief; 600 dpi adds nothing).

**Sources of truth** (read these before planning anything): [`docs/estado-e-plano.md`](docs/estado-e-plano.md) (state with evidence, root cause, milestones, metrics, photos needed, backlog) and [`ARCHITECTURE.md`](ARCHITECTURE.md) (real module map, contracts, entry points, data). `docs/revisao.md` holds the review thread. Diagnostic evidence and reproducible scripts: `docs/diagnostico/` and `tools/diagnostico/`. The README is outdated and only carries a pointer.

## Current Objective (decided 2026-10-29, revised 2026-10-30)

**Primary: find the twin.** For a piece placed at (l, c), find its twin among the loose scanned pieces (the cell (48 − l, 65 − c)): shape (side chords, 180°-invariant free distance) plus topological class, with the **image tie-break mandatory** (the twin's cell is known, so it is a 1-against-1 test against the art, not a search). Pairs inside the batch and an incremental index (placed pieces leave the pool) keep the effective pool far below 3008. The shape alone is viable up to about 60 candidates and not enough at 3008 (margin extrapolation in §6 of the plan). Product flow (§0 of the plan): incremental scans → persistent piece index + board map → suggestions ordered twin → hole mould → box (textured only) → loose-loose fit → confirmation in the GUI updates the map and spawns new queries.

**First real use (E-real-2, after M10):** the assembled grass strip (bottom rows) has its twins in the sky (top rows, mirrored columns); scan loose sky pieces and place them through their grass twins. E-real-2 ran 2026-10-31 with the scans of the BE corner (20 grass interiors) and 100 sky pieces (`images/pieces/gt_canto_BE.md`; `docs/diagnostico/gemeos/EREAL2.md`): 2 proposals (1 accepted, 1 ambiguous, both sky and mutual), 0 shape false positives in 10 472 known-negative pairs, image consistent with both but weakly discriminative in sky; awaiting Tiago's physical confirmation (`ereal2_propostas.txt`). Known defect S3: a piece touching the white paper strip at the scan edge is dropped (2 of 42 in f2p1).

**Product rule (2026-10-31):** every piece is scanned loose before it is placed, so the index already holds the silhouette of every placed piece; cut-line extraction is only for sky sections and legacy; legacy grass is re-scanned loose in blocks (exploded view). **Usage model:** Tiago scans random lots of ~32 pieces, each lot kept in a numbered bag; per new piece, in order: placed twin → twin among loose pieces (pair + joint image localisation) → hole/frontier → image (textured only) → stays in memory; each confirmation re-evaluates the memory and emits a "fetch lot N, piece X" list with the piece image.

**Secondary: assisted assembly by piece fit and hole mould.** Given an already-assembled section (photo/scan) and a batch of loose pieces (scan), rank which loose pieces attach to each free side of the section's frontier and where, and identify the piece that belongs in a hole (the right one or its twin). The side is the unit; the die has 5905 internal cuts, about 2953 distinct under the symmetry (interactive query, O(N) per query; no global 3000-piece solver). The assembled section is the local high-resolution reference.

**Tertiary: locating a loose piece in the box image** (the old primary goal) is kept only as a weak prior for textured pieces, reported with honest confidence. Measured: 72 % top-1 on synthetic self-crops, 0/61 confirmable on real scanned pieces. Cause (corrected 2026-10-30): the scanner is faithful to the art (uniform L\* shift of about +5), and the 61 July pieces really are light sky/cloud pieces without texture (art limit) compared against a box photo with a margin; photometric normalization (M9) reduces to a shift of about −5 L\*. The 61 July pieces were pre-filtered by Tiago (the ones he could not place): the L\* 86 is selection bias and the 0/61 is a worst-case sample; performance on random pieces is untested.

**Priorities, in order** (acceptance criteria in `docs/estado-e-plano.md` §7):
1. **M5 ✅ (E-sym, decided 2026-10-30)** — twins by 180° rotation: viable for the puzzle, not viable with the current segmentation. Hybrid contour + free metric: twin 0.29 / 0.39 mm (same / opposite pose), top-1 20/20 in both poses, 40/40 in a pool of 100; with the `segment_pieces` boundary: 1.01–1.42 mm, top-1 13/20 and 8/20 (the cause is the boundary, not the metric). Evidence in `docs/diagnostico/gemeos/`.
2. **M1** — ground-truth dataset: the 40 border pieces of the E-sym scans (GT by cell) + the cells of the assembled section with (l, c) (`seccao_celulas.json`, front scan only) + interior twin pairs (§8.1.0 g) + hole-mould blocks; the 5×8 block comes later.
3. **M2–M4** — reference cropped to the artwork with the known cell size (gives the twin's cell); segmentation serving geometry (E-fundo done 2026-10-31 (green wins: 0.44 mm cross-background RMS, 61/61 recall; hue-based shadow rejection is the cleanest contour on light pieces but fails E-sym on dark grass pieces, 0.77 mm / 15 of 20, so the M3 boundary still needs a shadow model restricted to the outer 1.2 mm band on the shadow-facing sides) and S1 fixed; boundary without bites on dark pieces and with the cast shadow handled, so that E-sym with the `segment_pieces` contour is at least as good as the hybrid one: twin ≤ 0.5 mm, top-1 20/20; piece near the scan edge not dropped; cut-line extraction on assembled sections from the front scan, target ≥ 90 % of cells without a prior; fringes, mated pairs, side-length repeatability ≤ 0.3 mm); `src/shape.py` (corners, sides, topology, shape ID, side as unit, cut dictionary), replacing the border classification of `edges.py`. The scan-boundary part of M3 is done via `segment_scan` (2026-10-31); cut-line extraction stays only for sky sections and legacy (product rule); textured-art detector out of M3, and the shadow model is deferred, conditional on E-real showing bias.
4. **M12** `src/state.py` (persistent piece index, board map, append-only confirmation log; §10 of the plan) between M4 and M10; then **M10** — `src/twin.py`: `find_twin` (class filter, chord filter of about 1 mm per side, gap, mandatory image tie-break, calibrated rejection, persistent index); conditional on M3; M10 does not close without **E-real** (cut-line cell contour of the assembled section → twin among loose pieces). E-real ran 2026-10-31 (`docs/diagnostico/gemeos/EREAL.md`): inconclusive, top-1 22/25 (20/21 on cells interior to the block); the silhouette sits an isotropic +0.48 mm outside the cut-line cell (not shadow: asymmetry ≤ 0.09 mm), so `find_twin` must calibrate that offset per modality; cut-line extraction on textured art (grass) failed (1–3/41 without prior), which is the open M3 item. Then **M11** (hole mould, first half of `src/fit.py`), M7 (section localisation: known corner and scale, SIFT only on textured zones), M6 (frontier fit, second half of `fit.py`), M8 (GUI) and last M9 (photometric shift). **M13** (puzzle profile `puzzle.json` + per-puzzle folder, landing with M12; grid/symmetry calibration routine after M10) follows M12.

M0 (measurement base: tests green on OpenCV 4.11 and 5.0, pinned requirements, cleanup) is done (`7c9fa89`). Order = priority; milestone IDs are historical (see §7 of the plan).

**Out of scope**: ML classification, REST API, GPU, live camera, perspective correction (the scanner removes it), global solver, more background-colour experiments, random-cut / grid-less puzzles.

**Generalization**: nothing puzzle-specific in code; grid, cell, symmetry rule, background and dpi live in a puzzle profile (`puzzle.json`, `projects/<id>/`), see §11 of the plan.

**Rule of evidence**: progress is measured with ground truth (metrics in §6 of the plan), never with the count of `high` labels. Every CV change follows the `cv-visual-verification` skill (look at the images, report before/after numbers, keep `pytest tests/` green).

## Subagents & Model Routing

Three subagents are defined in `.claude/agents/`:

| Subagent | Model | Role | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **planner** | Fable | Architecture and deep-planning specialist. Read-only tools (Read, Grep, Glob, WebSearch, WebFetch). | Never writes or edits code — plans and decisions only. Invoked ONLY for genuinely complex problems the implementer cannot chart alone (e.g. choosing between fundamentally different algorithmic strategies, cross-module architecture decisions). Expensive and weekly-limited — last resort, used as rarely as possible. None of the current milestones needs it. |
| **implementer** | Opus | Primary workhorse. Full editing tools. | Handles complex logic needing real reasoning but with a clear path: image algorithms, shape/fit geometry, segmentation, scoring/metrics, non-trivial bug fixes. Owns all changes to the critical modules (`src/matching.py`, `src/segmentation.py`, the future `src/shape.py`/`src/fit.py`/`src/twin.py`) and reviews the helper's algorithmic edits. Default tier for substantive coding work. |
| **helper** | Sonnet | Cheapest, first-choice tier. Full editing tools. | Handles simple, low-risk work: GUI/UI tweaks, mechanical refactors, formatting, renames, documentation, requirements edits, simple tests, small fixes. Hard limit: must NOT change algorithmic logic in the critical modules without the implementer designing or reviewing the change. Escalates instead of guessing. |

### Routing chain and rules

- Routing chain, cheapest first: **helper (Sonnet) → implementer (Opus) → planner (Fable)**.
- Always start at the cheapest tier that can plausibly do the task. Only escalate to the implementer when the helper is not enough. Only escalate to the planner when the implementer hits a genuine architecture/strategy wall it cannot resolve itself.
- Subagents cannot spawn other subagents. "Escalation" means the subagent stops and reports clearly what it needs and why, so the main orchestrator routes the work to the right tier.
- Hand-off pattern: planner produces a plan → implementer executes it → helper does the surrounding simple/mechanical work. The implementer reviews any helper edits that touch critical modules.
- Concrete mapping for the current milestones: data wrangling, tooling, docs and GUI wiring → helper; analyses that define a metric (E-sym, cut lines, margin extrapolation), M2–M4, M10, M11 and M6 algorithms → implementer; planner not needed.

## Cost-Saving Principle

The user has a tight **weekly limit on the Fable model**, so Fable must be used sparingly — only for planning/deep analysis of genuinely complex problems. The bulk of the work must fall on Opus (complex implementation) and Sonnet (simple tasks).

- Always use the cheapest model sufficient for the task.
- Reserve Fable (planner) strictly for complex problem planning; invoke it as little as possible.
- Let Opus (implementer) and Sonnet (helper) carry the bulk of the work.
- This is the user's primary cost-saving mechanism and must be respected in all work.

## Conventions

- `src/` uses tab indentation, `__all__` exports, and docstrings; pure helpers are kept separate from I/O; functions return `{'error': str, ...}` on failure (contract of `matching.py`/`segmentation.py`).
- Critical modules requiring implementer design/review for any algorithmic change: `src/matching.py`, `src/segmentation.py`, and the geometry/fit modules to be created (`src/shape.py`, `src/fit.py`, `src/twin.py`).
- Reports to the user label every claim as verified (with evidence), not tested, or tested-and-failing; external claims carry a link. Commit messages in Portuguese, no Co-Authored-By line.
